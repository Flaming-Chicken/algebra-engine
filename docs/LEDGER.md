# URAE Defect and Stub Ledger

*Checked 19 Nov 2026 against `dev` after v0.6.0 (unreleased changes are in the changelog). This file is the single place that says whether a known defect, stub or oversimplification is open or fixed, and what the evidence is. Plans and designs live in [`PLANNING.md`](PLANNING.md) and [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md); the long catalogues the ids come from are in [`audits/`](audits/).*

## 1. How to read this ledger

**Ids.** `A1–A16` silent stubs, `B1–B20` formula and algorithm defects, `C1–C17` oversimplifications: the catalogue in [`audits/CODEBASE_AUDIT_AND_REMEDIATION_PLAN.md`](audits/CODEBASE_AUDIT_AND_REMEDIATION_PLAN.md). `R1–R13` are findings made while remediating (not in the catalogue). `D1–D14`, `E1–E8`, `F1–F16`, `G1–G8`, `H1–H5`, `I1–I6`, `J1–J6` are the catalogue's later tracks (section 6). An id is never reused: `R11–R13` were called `A12–A14` until 19 Nov 2026, when the catalogue took those ids for different items, and `R1–R10` were called `D1–D10` before Track `D` claimed that prefix.

**Status vocabulary.** *Fixed*: the cited behaviour is gone and a regression test fails on the old code. *Partly fixed*: the entry says what remains. *Open*: the behaviour was seen on `dev` (*reproduced*: a test was run and gave the wrong answer; *read*: the cited code shows it). *Stale*: the claim no longer matches the code. *Not verified*: not examined; never counted as a defect.

- **C4 Partly fixed (gear, airfoil, spring, screw).** A closure audit of every CAD builder (`Mesh3D::boundary_edge_count` / `is_closed`) found the gear (real bore, annular caps), airfoil and spring (end caps) and the screw were not solids. All four are now closed, outward-oriented meshes with checked volumes. The screw (`ThreadedScrew::generate_3d_mesh`) is one height-field grid over polar angle and height: a helical thread of radius `thread_radius` (ISO 60°, Acme 29° or square profile, periodic in the pitch), a plain shank, a hex or round head and fan end faces; its volume equals `π·L·mean(r²) + shank + head` to within 1% (96 angles, 24 rows per pitch). The gear outline was also wrong (flanks started at the base circle with no involute offset, so teeth were thinner than pi*m/2); it is now built from true involutes with correct pitch-circle thickness and a profile-shift coefficient (`InvoluteGear::with_profile_shift`, thickness pi*m/2 + 2*x*m*tan(alpha), tip/root radii shifted). `CycloidalGear` (epicycloid addendum and hypocycloid dedendum from a rolling circle on the pitch circle, `rolling_ratio` of the pitch radius; tooth thickness `pi m / 2` on the pitch circle, checked geometrically; the outline is star-shaped and winds exactly once; with a rolling circle of half the pitch radius the dedendum flank is a radial line, as theory says) builds on the same closed-solid generator as the involute gear (`toothed_gear_solid`, now shared). ## 7. R. Findings made while remediating

Each of these was found by auditing the code or the tests while fixing something else. They are all fixed; the entry says how it was found, what was wrong, and how it is checked.

- **R1 remainder (open).** Arbitrary CAD surface meshes (`CadFeaBridge::mesh3d_to_fea`) still use the 3-D `Tri3` diagonal pseudo-stiffness; gears, wings, springs and screws are real tetrahedral solids.
- **R1 (found in the FEA stress audit) Fixed for 2-D and Tet4.** The plane-strain constitutive matrix was wrong (`C11 = E/((1+v)(1-2v))`, `C12` divided by `1-v`, shear modulus `(1-v)/2` of that), and the per-element von Mises stress was built from absolute values of nodal differences (`|u1-u0| + |u2-u0|`), which is not a strain at all. - **R2 (found building the gear solid mesh) Fixed.** The gear end-cap triangulation (angular merge of the toothed outline with the bore ring) emitted one *folded* triangle per radial root segment on small pinions (12 of them on a 12-tooth gear): an outline edge that is nearly radial has the axis almost on its line, so a bore vertex angularly behind it lies on the wrong side. The folds cancelled in the signed volume, so every volume test passed. The merge now checks the orientation of the candidate triangles and advances the bore first when the angular rule would fold; a new test requires every cap triangle of involute, cycloidal and internal gears to have the correct orientation. Built on that, `CadFeaBridge::gear_to_fea` now meshes a bored gear as a real **solid** of linear tetrahedra (cap triangles extruded through the layers, each prism cut into three tetrahedra with the index-sorted diagonal rule; FEA nodes are the CAD vertices; tetrahedra volumes sum exactly to the solid's volume; bore reactions balance the tooth load), so the pipeline's gear analysis is real 3-D elasticity rather than the surface pseudo-stiffness. Limits: a gear without a bore and a strongly twisted helical gear (or any twisted gear with radial root segments, i.e. fewer than about 42 teeth) keep the surface fallback, because the stacked prisms would fold; airfoils are now solids too (the centroid-fan section cap is extruded through the 8 spanwise sections by the same shared prism stacker; the FEA nodes are the CAD vertices plus one centroid node per interior section; tetrahedra volumes equal the wing's volume, a clamped wing under a tip load deflects towards the load, more towards the tip, and the root reactions balance it; a twisted wing is a solid as well because rotated sections never fold). Springs are solids too (`CadFeaBridge::spring_to_fea`, groups `spring_bottom` / `spring_top`): the wire's circular cross-section (a fan around the ring centre) is swept along the helix by the same prism stacker, with an orientation guard that uses the triangle normal along the path since the rings are not planar-parallel; tetrahedra volumes agree with the closed mesh to 3%, deflection is linear in the load, the clamp carries the whole load, and the axial stiffness is 1.5 to 3 times the coil formula `G d^4 / (8 D^3 n)` (linear tetrahedra over an 8-element wire are too stiff in torsion, so it is an upper bound). Screws are solids too (without a key socket): the CAD mesh is a grid of star-shaped rows, so the fan cross-section is extruded through the rows by the same prism stacker; a radius step (a pair of rows at one height, which would give zero-volume tetrahedra) becomes a thin cone by lowering the lower row by `0.05 pitch`, adding under 3% to the volume (checked). A tensioned M8 bolt's stiffness lies between the minor-area and major-area bar formulas and the clamp reaction equals the load. A screw with a key socket is a solid too: every layer carries an inner ring at the socket outline, the fan to the axis is meshed only below the cavity floor and the annulus (outline to socket) all the way up, so the cavity is a void whose floor is the fan's top face and the two stacks conform because they share base triangles; nodes in the void are dropped. The socketed M8 bolt is softer than the plain one in tension but never stiffer, and the volume matches the CAD solid within 4%.
- **R3 (found auditing mesh orientation) Fixed.** `Mesh3D::compute_volume` returns an absolute value, so a mesh whose normals point *into* the solid passes every closure and volume check. - **R5 (found routing the old Zeilberger types through the new module) Fixed.** The hard-coded `ZeilbergerAlgorithm::prove_binomial_sum` shipped a wrong certificate: `G = k/(n+1-k) C(n,k)` has the opposite sign (the relation `C(n+1,k) - 2 C(n,k) = G(k+1) - G(k)` needs `G = -k/(n+1-k) C(n,k) = -C(n,k-1)`), and `prove_vandermonde` shipped `R = k (b-n+k) / ((n+1-k)(a+b-n))` where the correct certificate, found by solving for `G/F` exactly, is `R = -k (b-n+k) / (n+1-k)` (an extra `1/(a+b-n)` factor and the wrong sign). Below 2^127 the rho iteration (Brent's cycle detection with batched gcds) now runs in Montgomery form on `u128` (a 128 x 128 -> 256 bit product built from `u64` limbs, `Montgomery::mul`), with a larger step budget: 81 bits 0.29 s, 89 bits 0.64 s, 97 bits 1.3 s, 105 bits 18 s (debug build; every factorisation was multiplied back and each factor tested prime). A perfect square `p^2` (which rho cannot split) is now handled by an exact integer square root. The Montgomery arithmetic is checked against `BigUint` on 100 pseudo-random 128-bit products and 600 modular products over moduli of 3 to 127 bits (`numbertheory::montgomery_tests`) and the factorisations in `factoring_speed_tests`. Above 127 bits the old `BigUint` path remains, and rho is still limited to factors of about 50 bits (a 52-bit prime factor already takes 18 s); an elliptic-curve method would be the next step.
- **Known gap closed: polynomial times a power of a linear form.** `integrate(x*sqrt(x+1))` (and `x^2*(x+1)^(3/2)`, `x/sqrt(x+1)`, `x*sqrt(x)`, ...) was an error ("multi-factor product integration not matched" or "Hermite factorization required"); only a bare `(a x + b)^e` integrated. `RischIntegrator::integrate` now first tries an exact substitution `u = a x + b` for `P(x) * (a x + b)^e` with `P` a polynomial with rational coefficients and `e` a non-integer rational (`sqrt` is `e = 1/2`; a quotient by such a power has `-e`; a rational constant may divide): `P` is rewritten as a polynomial in `u` and each `q_m u^m` integrates to `q_m u^(m+e+1) / (a (m+e+1))`, with no zero denominator because `e` is not an integer. Fourteen integrands (including `(3x^3 - x + 2) sqrt(2x + 5)` and `(x^2 + 1)/(x - 4)^(1/3)`) are checked by differentiating the answer back at four points; outside the family (`x sqrt(x^2 + 1)`, a product of two radicals) the answer is still a refusal or a correct antiderivative. Not covered: radicals of a non-linear base (`x sqrt(x^2 + 1)` is elementary but still refused).
- **A8 follow-up: all-n proofs (`zeilberger_proof`).** The recurrences found by `zeilberger` were established only at the sampled `n`. `prove_recurrence` now proves them for every `n`: the certificate `R = G/F` is rebuilt exactly at each sample (from the Gosper normal form and the solved `x(k)`), reduced to lowest terms and each coefficient reconstructed as a rational function of `n` by exact interpolation, giving a candidate `R = U(n, k)/V(n, k)` with polynomial `U`, `V`; then the telescoping identity is checked as an identity of bivariate polynomials (`Poly2`, with shifts in `n` and `k`), with no sampling. A wrong guess fails the symbolic check and is an error. Proved this way: the binomial row sum (`S(n+1) = 2 S(n)`), `sum C(n,k)^2` (`(n+1) S(n+1) = (4n+2) S(n)`) and the central Delannoy numbers (order 2); a wrong recurrence and a right recurrence attached to the wrong term are rejected. What is not proved is the boundary behaviour (that `G` vanishes at both ends of the sum), which holds for finite-support terms such as binomials and is the caller's to know.
- **Solver audit (no defect found).** Twenty-seven equations with known roots (repeated, rational and irrational polynomial roots, `sqrt(x+2) = x` with its extraneous root, absolute values, a removable singularity, equations with no solution) were solved and every answer was checked by substitution and against the expected real roots; complex roots (`x^2+1`, `x^4+4`, `exp(x)+1`) come back as exact complex values and a transcendental equation (`2^x = x + 1`) as an honest `RootOf`. The real-root cases are now a regression test (`solve_known_roots_tests`).
